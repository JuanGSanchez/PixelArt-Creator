Basic Font Information
----------------------

Nifen Art CJK is the second face of the Nifen Art pixel typeface. It holds the Han
characters and the Hangul syllables that the first face leaves out, so that interface
text in Japanese, Simplified Chinese and Korean can be set in the same pixel style. It
is made of three families, each in two files (Regular and Bold) that form one
style-linked family:

- Nifen Art CJK JP: Han characters in the Japanese forms;
- Nifen Art CJK SC: Han characters in the Simplified Chinese forms;
- Nifen Art CJK KR: Hangul syllables.

Design facts:

- The grid is the one of Nifen Art. One em is 12 pixels, every pixel is a square of
  128 font units, so the em is 1536 units. The glyphs are meant to be shown at 12 px, or
  at whole multiples of 12 px, without antialiasing.
- Every glyph is full-width: its advance is one 12 pixel cell, and its ink takes at most
  11 columns and 11 rows of that cell (columns 0 to 10, rows -1 to 9).
- The line box is the one of Nifen Art (11 pixels above the baseline and 3 below), so
  that a line does not change height when it moves from one face to the other.
- Bold is Regular. In every family the Bold file holds the same outlines as the Regular
  file, pixel for pixel. The two files differ in their names and style flags only. The
  Bold kana of Nifen Art are heavier than these characters; that is accepted.
- The character map of a family holds Han characters (Japanese and Simplified families)
  or Hangul syllables (Korean family), and nothing else: no space, no punctuation, no
  kana and no Latin letters. Those stay in Nifen Art.

Character coverage:

- A family holds exactly the characters of its kind that the application's text uses:
  its interface texts, its user guide pages and the names of the languages in the
  language selector. It is not a standard character list. The glyphs are drawn in
  batches as the text is written, and a character that no family holds is shown in a
  system font.
- Each Han form set is measured from its own language's text. A language name is drawn
  in the forms of its own language whatever the interface language is.

Design notes:

- Every glyph is an original drawing made for this grid, or is composed from other
  glyphs drawn for this project. No glyph, outline or metric was copied or adapted from
  another font.
- The forms of the Han characters follow the glyph forms of the national standards of
  each language. A standard decides which form a drawing shows; it supplies no bitmap.
- At 11 by 11 pixels a stroke is one pixel wide in Regular. A glyph may hold a block of
  2 by 2 pixels only where the sources declare it, with a reason.
- The fonts are generated from plain-text glyph grids by a deterministic build:
  rebuilding the same sources gives byte-identical files.

ChangeLog
---------

24 October 2026 (JuanGSanchez)
  Nifen Art CJK version 0.100, work in progress.
  - The CJK face is added as a second face of the typeface: three families (Japanese
    Han forms, Simplified Han forms, Hangul), Regular and Bold, with Bold equal to
    Regular.
  - The glyph sets are filled in batches, starting with the language names of the
    language selector.
